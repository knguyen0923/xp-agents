# XP — short write-up: when does uncertainty-driven exploration pay off?

## Question
Two agents share a learner and differ by one term. **Kairi** is model-free and reward-driven (epsilon-greedy in a bandit, plain PPO in MiniGrid). **Kaiser** is Kairi plus an explicit
uncertainty-driven exploration term (discounted UCB in the bandit, PPO + an RND bonus in MiniGrid), the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty. We ask where each wins and
where each breaks as the world moves away from the conditions both were tuned on. Kairi winning some regimes is an expected, valid result.

## Protocol
Tune each agent on the familiar knob value with tune seeds only, freeze, evaluate at every knob value on disjoint eval seeds, and report degradation curves, paired differences and
crossovers with 95% bootstrap CIs (resampling the independent seed). Both agents get equal-sized tuning grids. A tuned optimum on a grid edge makes a run provisional, and provisional
results are never reported; we widened grids and reran when it happened (reversed-tuning and drift shifts). Runs are resumable and keyed by config hash.

## Results
**Changing bandit (10 arms, sigma 1, horizon 2000, tuned at jump rate 0.001).** Kaiser beats Kairi at all seven jump rates (regret per step 0.033 to 0.072 lower, every CI excludes 0).
The advantage holds when tuning at the high jump rate and across reward noise 0.25 to 4. Kairi wins in two shifts far from the tuned point with frozen settings: 50 arms (by 0.054 [0.037, 0.071]) and arm drift 0.1 per step (by 0.209 [0.132, 0.283]), crossovers between 20 and 50 arms and between
drift 0.02 and 0.1. A post-hoc ablation retuning both agents at those points reverses both: Kaiser then wins by 0.086 [0.070, 0.103] at 50 arms and 0.168 [0.156, 0.179] at drift 0.1, and a regret-over-time split
shows Kaiser's frozen loss at 50 arms accrues after step 500, not from initial exploration. So the Kairi wins are Kaiser's tuned settings being brittle, not exploration hurting. Tag-team agents that switch between the
two help mainly where the single agents fail; `tt_meta` is the most reliable.

**MiniGrid DoorKey (tuned on 6x6, tested at 5, 6, 8, 12, 10 seeds).** With tuned settings Kairi is at or above Kaiser everywhere, and the gap excludes 0 at sizes 8 and 12. That gap is not robust:
Kaiser at a different tuning-tied RND coefficient, or at Kairi's entropy coefficient, is statistically indistinguishable from Kairi at every size. The supported conclusion is no measurable benefit
from RND on DoorKey, not that it hurts. One Kaiser seed failed outright in two of three RND settings, so seed-level failures matter. A post-hoc MultiRoom spike (28 runs, 2 seeds, 2M steps) on an exploration-heavy family found no RND benefit at that budget, but a follow-up probe at 5M steps on the borderline level (4 small rooms, 5 seeds) found that RND at coefficient 0.1 reaches 95% training success in a median 1.44M steps against 2.66M for plain PPO, faster on all 5 seeds (mean 1.17M fewer steps, CI [0.53M, 1.75M]); coefficients 0.01 and 0.03 gave no benefit. This is post-hoc (best of three coefficients, top of the range tried, tune-pool seeds), so it is evidence, not a protocol result. A protocol-legal confirmation at that level (coefficients tuned on 3 tune seeds, Kaiser's entropy matched to Kairi's, 10 disjoint eval seeds, S = 2.94M steps) then found Kaiser (`rnd_coef` 0.6) faster on all 10 seeds: mean 0.70M against 2.23M steps to 95% (paired CI of the difference [−2.12M, −0.93M]), and 10/10 seeds solved against 5/10 for Kairi. One level only; the level and grid were chosen after the probe; the step and AUC gaps are the robust numbers because Kairi's failures depend on the budget.

**Pokémon Showdown (VGC doubles vs DamageHeuristic, one familiar level, S = 250k decisions, 10 eval seeds).** A tie. Training AUC 0.406 [0.397, 0.413] for Kairi (tuned `ent_coef` 0.01) and 0.403 [0.394, 0.410] for Kaiser (tuned `rnd_coef` 0.03), paired difference −0.003 [−0.015, +0.008]; final win rate 0.455 vs 0.451; Kairi reaches a 30% win rate about 5k decisions sooner but the interval [−1k, +11k] includes 0. One opponent and no shift knob, so this says the bonus did not hurt or help here, not where either breaks. **With type-matchup inputs added to the observation** (a second run with wider, re-tuned grids: Kairi `ent_coef` 0.006, Kaiser `rnd_coef` 1.0) it is still a tie: AUC 0.416 [0.408, 0.425] vs 0.424 [0.417, 0.431], difference (Kaiser − Kairi) +0.008 [−0.006, +0.021]. Against the original run, paired by eval seed, only Kaiser's AUC rises clearly (+0.022 [+0.012, +0.031], 9 of 10 seeds); the other changes include 0 and the gain is not reliably larger for Kaiser (+0.011 [−0.001, +0.026]). A control run (the original inputs on the same wider grids; Kairi `ent_coef` 0.002, Kaiser `rnd_coef` 0.03; AUC 0.409 vs 0.410, a tie) separates the two for Kaiser: wider grids and retuning alone +0.008 [−0.004, +0.018], adding the inputs +0.014 [+0.008, +0.020] (9 of 10 seeds). Kairi's AUC moves by +0.004 then +0.007, both intervals including 0. **Tempo follow-up (2026-11-10):** against a scripted opponent that sets Trick Room, Tailwind and screens, with a friendly-fire reward penalty, two runs both tie. Run 1 (no tempo observation block; Kairi `ent_coef` 0.006, Kaiser `rnd_coef` 1.0): AUC 0.361 [0.355, 0.365] vs 0.360 [0.355, 0.364], difference (Kairi − Kaiser) +0.0007 [−0.007, +0.009]. Run 2 (tempo block added; Kairi `ent_coef` 0.02, Kaiser `rnd_coef` 0.03): AUC 0.353 [0.347, 0.360] vs 0.356 [0.347, 0.364], difference −0.003 [−0.012, +0.007]. Paired by eval seed, run 2 minus run 1 is −0.007 [−0.017, +0.002] for Kairi and −0.004 [−0.013, +0.005] for Kaiser; every interval includes 0, and the tuned values changed between the runs, so that includes retuning. Tables: `docs/spikes/showdown_tempo_results/`.

## Limitations
One bandit family and horizon; agents are frozen after tuning once (no per-shift retuning); no multiplicity correction across knob values; the ablations that explain the Kairi wins are post-hoc, cover only those two shifts, and use oracle retuning the protocol does not allow. MiniGrid: one familiar size, a 2M-step budget that missed the 80% calibration target, 10 eval seeds, and Kaiser's entropy
coefficient was fixed (not tuned) in the main run, which the matched-entropy check addresses post-hoc. Showdown: one opponent, 10 seeds, a fixed 250k-decision budget, and a noisy Kaiser tuning pick (the matchup run's AUC gain for Kaiser is split by a control run into a grids-and-retuning step that includes 0 and an inputs step that does not, from 10 seeds and nine uncorrected cross-run comparisons; the tempo runs add 10 seeds, one scripted opponent, and tuned values that changed between the two runs, so their cross-run comparison includes retuning). Tuning grids: Kairi and Kaiser tune different hyperparameters in equal-sized grids (49 points in the bandit), and the edge rule only catches optima at a grid boundary, not coarse resolution. Two caveats from the tuning tables: in the familiar bandit condition Kairi's `epsilon` barely changes the score, so its 49-point grid is effectively one-dimensional (`alpha`) while Kaiser's is a genuine two-dimensional valley, which may favor Kaiser; and in `bandit-drift` Kaiser's optimum sits at the natural limit `gamma=1.0` with its nearest neighbor 40% worse. Elsewhere the best point's neighbors are within 1 to 10%, so resolution is a small risk. A finer Kairi `alpha` sweep (13 alpha values by the same 7 epsilons, tune seeds only; `scripts/bandit_alpha_sweep.py`, table `docs/bandit_alpha_sweep/kairi_fine_alpha.csv`) was run on 2026-10-31: the best tuning regret improved from 0.2379 (epsilon 0.02, alpha 0.4) to 0.2236 (alpha 0.28), against Kaiser's 0.1724, so the coarse grid cost Kairi about 6% and left the gap to Kaiser at about 23%; the grid resolution does not explain Kaiser's advantage in the familiar condition. Eval-seed results were not rerun with the finer grid. A post-hoc check on the eval seeds (`scripts/bandit_drift_check.py`, `docs/bandit_drift_check/kaiser_neighbours.csv`, 2026-11-01) confirms the drift optimum: three neighbours of Kaiser's tuned point are all worse (+0.0075 [0.0040, 0.0110] at `gamma` 0.999, `c` 0.5, the closest; +0.0238 and +0.0206 for the other two), so the valley is shallow along its ridge and steep across it. See `docs/FINDINGS.md`.
