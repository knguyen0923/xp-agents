How the test works

Each agent is tuned on the familiar world using tuning seeds only, then frozen. It is then run on separate evaluation seeds at every world setting. The seed pools never overlap, and the code refuses to run if they do. We report paired 95% bootstrap intervals, resampling seeds. A tuned value sitting on the edge of its grid marks the run provisional, and a provisional run never reaches a final chart. Kairi winning somewhere is a valid result.

Chapter 1: in a changing bandit, Kaiser wins, and the two fail at the same distance

The bandit is a pull-an-arm world where the best arm moves. Kairi uses epsilon-greedy; Kaiser uses discounted UCB. Each panel shows regret per step (lower is better) as one setting of the world is pushed away from the tuned value. Shaded bands are 95% intervals.

Kaiser has lower regret nearly everywhere. The two exceptions are the largest number of arms and the fastest drift, the two panels where Kairi's line dips below Kaiser's. Both are frozen-settings effects, shown next.

Chapter 2: both Kairi wins disappear when the agents are retuned

We reran the protocol with the tuning point moved to the shifted world. Each dot is Kairi minus Kaiser regret: above zero means Kaiser is better. Bars are 95% intervals. This is an after-the-fact check and uses information the real protocol does not allow, so it explains the wins rather than replacing them.

Retuned, Kaiser wins at the points where Kairi had won. The loss came from Kaiser's frozen settings fitting the old world, not from exploring being harmful there. Practical reading: retune Kaiser when the world moves a long way.

Chapter 3: on DoorKey, the exploration bonus gave no measurable benefit

DoorKey is a MiniGrid key-then-door task, tuned at size 6 and tested at 5, 6, 8 and 12 (10 seeds each, success rate shown). With Kaiser's tuned settings Kairi looks ahead at sizes 8 and 12. That gap does not survive two checks: using a different tuning-tied bonus strength, or giving Kaiser the same entropy coefficient as Kairi, makes the two statistically indistinguishable. A plausible but untested reason: plain exploration already reaches the reward, so the bonus has little to add.

Chapter 4: where exploration is the bottleneck, RND finally helps

That pointed to a level where reaching the goal is hard. MultiRoom with 4 small rooms fits: the agent must find a chain of doors. First, a quick unplanned probe (5 seeds, three bonus strengths) hinted that a strong bonus reaches the goal faster. That probe picked its best value after seeing results, so it is a hint and not evidence.

Chapter 5: the confirmation, run under the full protocol

We then ran the same level the legal way. Kairi's entropy was tuned first on 3 tuning seeds; Kaiser took that entropy and tuned only its bonus strength over six values from 0.01 to 1.0. The training budget was calibrated so default Kairi just reaches 95% on the tuning seeds. Both tuned values landed inside their grids.

Tuning: training AUC of success per setting

Higher is better. Bars are the mean of 3 tuning seeds, dots are single seeds, the chosen setting is filled. Chosen: Kairi entropy —, Kaiser bonus —.

Kairi's pick is noisy: with 3 seeds and all-or-nothing outcomes, its winner rests on a couple of take-off times. Kaiser's larger bonus values all learn fast, so its pick is steadier.

Chapter 6: Crafter, a world we tried and set aside

Crafter is a much larger world: the agent sees an image and can unlock 22 achievements, from collecting wood to making an iron pickaxe. Before spending days on it, we ran a spike with plain PPO only (no exploration bonus), one seed at each of two world sizes, 1M steps each. The test was set in advance: the agent had to average at least 5 distinct achievements per episode, because a near-random policy already averages about 3.

Plain PPO, achievements per episode (mean of the last 100 episodes)

Dashed line: the pass mark of 5. Both worlds were still climbing at 1M steps but ended below it.

Neither world reached the pass mark: 4.52 achievements in the small world and 4.37 in the large one. The agent learned wood, sapling, water, cow and zombie, but never placed a table, so the tools and iron stayed out of reach. The full study would have cost about 20 to 50 hours, so we closed Crafter as a documented spike. We may come back to it; it was set aside for time, and the question is still open. No exploration-bonus result was read.

What this does and does not show

  • Crafter is a spike, not a result. One seed per world size, plain PPO only. It shows the world is hard for plain PPO at 1M steps, not whether an exploration bonus would help.
  • One level. It says nothing about other room counts, and DoorKey still shows no benefit.
  • The design came after the probe. The level, target and bonus range were chosen after seeing it, and two tuning seeds overlap the probe seeds. Only the evaluation seeds are fresh.
  • Kairi's failures are budget failures. The budget was fitted to Kairi's tuning seeds, and in the probe plain PPO solved every seed given 5M steps. Trust the speed gaps (steps, AUC) more than the solved-or-not counts.
  • Steps, not time. The bonus adds compute per step; wall-clock was not compared.
  • Frozen settings are brittle. In the bandit, Kaiser's tuned values transfer worse than Kairi's. The advice is to retune it after a large shift.
